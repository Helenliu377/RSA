What Is a Construct?

A construct is an abstract concept that researchers want to study but cannot always observe or measure directly.

Brand Trust

How much consumers trust a brand.

Brand Attachment

How emotionally connected consumers feel to a brand.

Purchase Intention

How likely consumers are to buy a product.

Construct → Measurement

What we want to study → How we observe it.

From Construct to Measurement

Example: How complex does a supermarket aisle look to consumers?

CONSTRUCT

Visual Complexity

Perceived complexity of a retail aisle.

MEASUREMENT

5 Survey Items

Response scale: 1–6.

Visual Complexity Questionnaire

  1. Does this aisle seem complex to you?
  2. Is the spacing between each bottle on this shelf too tight?
  3. Do you find that there are many different bottles within this aisle?
  4. Is your eye more drawn to the center of the aisle?
  5. Do you think the aisle is well organized?

Response scale: 1 = Completely disagree · 6 = Completely agree

One construct can be measured using multiple questionnaire items.

What Is a Theory?

“A set of clearly identified variables and their connections, the reasons for those connections, and the primary boundary conditions for those connections.”

Cortina (2016).
WHAT

The key constructs

What are we studying?

HOW

The relationships

How are they connected?

WHY

The mechanism

Why should it happen?

WHO · WHERE · WHEN

Boundary conditions

When and where does it hold?

Example — Brand Experience

Positive experiences may strengthen emotional attachment because consumers develop meaningful connections with brands.

Illustrative mechanism for the WHY element

Cortina (2016), Journal of Organizational Behavior · Whetten (1989), Academy of Management Review.

What Theory Is Not

These elements can support a theory, but none of them is a theory on its own.

01

References

Citing who said it does not explain why it happens.

02

Data

Results describe what happened, not why.

03

Lists of Variables

Naming constructs is not relating them.

04

Diagrams

Boxes and arrows need the logic behind them.

05

Hypotheses

Predictions are what theory produces, not theory itself.

Theory is the WHY that connects them.

Your hypothesis section must explain why each relationship in your conceptual model should exist.

Sutton, R. I., & Staw, B. M. (1995). What theory is not. Administrative Science Quarterly, 40(3), 371–384.

A Good Hypothesis

  • ✓

    Testable with data you can collect.

  • ✓

    States a direction (+, −, stronger, higher).

  • ✓

    Has one IV and one DV, or one interaction.

  • ✓

    Uses a clear verb: affects, increases, reduces.

Avoid

  • ×

    Vague verbs: catalyses, triggers, boosts.

  • ×

    Two outcomes in one sentence.

  • ×

    No direction: "X influences Y".

  • ×

    Constructs absent from your research question.

Three Shapes of Hypothesis

DOES X AFFECT Y?

Direct Effect

H1: X has a positive effect on Y.

Test: t-test, ANOVA, or regression.

WHEN, OR FOR WHOM?

Moderated Effect

H2: The positive effect of X on Y is stronger when Z is high.

Test: factorial ANOVA or regression with an interaction term.

HOW, OR WHY?

Mediated Effect

H3: M mediates the positive effect of X on Y.

Advanced: Mediation analysis (e.g., PROCESS or JAMOVI).

X = Independent Variable · Y = Dependent Variable · Z = Moderator · M = Mediator

PART 3 · WRITING

Every Hypothesis Needs an Argument in Five Moves

Build a logical argument before stating your hypothesis.

01

Define

Recall the constructs involved, in one or two sentences.

02

Theorise

Explain the mechanism: why should X affect Y? This is your theory at work.

03

Evidence

Show what prior studies found in close settings, including conflicting results.

04

Apply

Say why the mechanism should hold in your context and population.

05

State

Write the formal hypothesis on its own line.

Length & Style

Length

Suggested writing guideline

150–300 words per hypothesis; one paragraph, one hypothesis.

Tense

Present tense for theory; past tense for specific findings: ‘Brakus et al. (2009) found…’

Citations

Cite only sources you have actually read and verified.

The goal is a convincing argument, not mechanically following five sentences.

PART 3 · WRITING

Worked Example: Five Moves, One Paragraph

Click a move to highlight its exact passage within the paragraph.

Brand experience refers to the sensations, feelings, cognitions and behavioural responses that brand-related stimuli evoke (Brakus et al., 2009); brand attachment is the strength of the bond connecting a brand with the self (Park et al., 2010). Attachment theory holds that such bonds grow out of repeated, emotionally significant interactions (Bowlby, 1979; Thomson et al., 2005): the more vivid a consumer's experiences with a brand, the more that brand becomes part of the self. Empirically, brand experience predicts satisfaction and loyalty (Brakus et al., 2009) and has been linked to brand attachment (Huaman-Ramirez & Merunka, 2019). On music-streaming platforms, consumers interact with the brand daily and across devices, which makes these experiences frequent and salient. Hence, we hypothesize that brand experience has a positive effect on brand attachment.
